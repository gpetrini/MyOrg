* Abstract

#+BEGIN_ABSTRACT
This paper estimates the cumulative Sraffian supermultiplier for the U.S. economy over the 1948 Q1–2024 Q4 period and tests whether it varies with labor market conditions.
Using the local projection estimator, it identifies autonomous demand shocks and assesses their dynamic effects on aggregate output over a five-year forecast horizon.
The linear supermultiplier is positive and statistically significant at 3- and 4-year horizons, with a declining profile over the forecast window, consistent with the gradual propagation predicted by the SSM multiplier-accelerator mechanism.
State-dependent results reveal substantial heterogeneity.
In periods of labor market slack, the supermultiplier exceeds unity and starts above 1.7; in tight labor market conditions, it falls rapidly toward zero.
The gap is statistically significant at the 3- and 4-year horizons.
These findings provide the first systematic dynamic evidence on the Sraffian supermultiplier and connect it to the state-dependent fiscal multiplier literature.
#+END_ABSTRACT
